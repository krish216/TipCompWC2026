## 1. Competitive Landscape

> Note: Live pricing and feature sets should be verified directly on each platform before agency briefing, as tournament-specific products may have launched or changed since this brief was prepared.

### 1.1 Superbru
**URL:** superbru.com
**Model:** Free to play; premium "Pro" tier (~$5–10/season) unlocks advanced stats and ad-free experience.
**Key features:** Pool-based tipping, social feeds per pool, push notifications, AFL/rugby/soccer support, mobile app (iOS + Android).
**Differentiator vs TribePicks:** Large established user base but pools are open-web social feeds — no meaningful privacy control for workplace comps. No "tip once, count everywhere" cross-comp logic, no favourite-team multiplier, no penalty shootout prediction layer.

### 1.2 Footytips (News Corp Australia)
**URL:** footytips.com.au
**Model:** Free; advertising-supported.
**Key features:** Strong in AFL/NRL tipping; World Cup support added for major tournaments. Expert tips visible to users.
**Differentiator vs TribePicks:** Australia-centric and editorially driven. Lacks tribe/sub-group structuring, no organiser tooling, built around ad inventory. No challenge/acquisition hook.

### 1.3 ESPN Tournament Challenge / ESPN Predictor
**URL:** espn.com/football
**Model:** Free; tied to ESPN account/ecosystem.
**Key features:** Global brand, high-production match data and editorial content, bracket-style and match predictor formats.
**Differentiator vs TribePicks:** Built for reach, not depth. No organiser layer, no private comp management, no bulk invitations, no reminders, no tribe chat, no configurable scoring. Functions as a single global leaderboard — workplace comps are impossible to administer.

### 1.4 Superstrike / White-label predictors
**Model:** Often B2B licensed to media partners; consumer-facing free play.
**Differentiator vs TribePicks:** No self-serve organiser tooling. Cannot sign up and create a branded comp for staff in under 5 minutes. No tribe structure, no reminders, no challenge hook.

### 1.5 UEFA / FIFA Official Predictor
**URL:** predictorgame.com / uefa.com
**Model:** Free; official tournament branding.
**Differentiator vs TribePicks:** Always global/public — no private groups. Scoring is fixed and non-configurable. No organiser tooling, no chat, no reminders, no challenge hook.

| Platform | Private Comps | Organiser Tools | Configurable Scoring | Tribe/Sub-groups | Acquisition Challenge | Shootout Tips |

**TribePicks' defensible white space:** The only product combining (a) fully private closed comps, (b) self-serve organiser tooling with bulk invites + reminders, (c) configurable scoring, (d) a no-signup acquisition challenge, and (e) penalty shootout predictions.

## 2. Target Audience Segments & Personas

### Persona 1 — "The Office Organiser"
**Name:** Marcus, 34 — HR Manager / People & Culture Lead, 200-person company
**Motivation:** Fun, low-effort staff engagement moment around the World Cup.
**Pain point:** Last tournament he managed a WhatsApp group and shared Google Sheet. Spent 20+ hours manually updating scores, chasing non-tippers, and settling rules arguments.
**How TribePicks solves it:** 10-minute setup, bulk email invites with tracking, automated reminders, live automatic scoring, announcement board, tribe sub-groups for dept vs dept.
**Primary channel:** LinkedIn, Google Search ("workplace World Cup tipping competition").

### Persona 2 — "The Casual Fan"
**Name:** Priya, 27 — Marketing Executive; follows her national team socially
**Motivation:** Be part of the World Cup buzz with friends and workmates.
**Pain point:** Gets invited to comps on unfamiliar platforms. Sign-up friction (download app → create account → verify email → find comp) means she drops off before tipping a single match.
**How TribePicks solves it:** /su-challenge lets her pick 4 matches with zero sign-up, see her projected rank, and feel the buzz before committing. Mobile-first web — no app download. She's hooked before she's registered.
**Primary channel:** Instagram, WhatsApp (shared challenge link), TikTok.

### Persona 3 — "The Football Obsessive"
**Name:** Jamie, 31 — Developer; tracks xG stats, has a favourite club and national team
**Motivation:** A prediction game that rewards football knowledge, not just luck. Multiple benchmarks.
**Pain point:** Most tipping games feel like a coin flip. No differentiation for deep knowledge — no shootout predictions, no favourite-team logic, no global benchmark. Bored after the group stage.
**How TribePicks solves it:** Penalty shootout predictions in knockout rounds. Favourite-team double points. Multi-level leaderboards (Tribe / Comp / Global). Engaged through the final.
**Primary channel:** Twitter/X, Reddit (r/soccer, r/worldcup), YouTube.

### Persona 4 — "The Tribe Captain"
**Name:** Aisha, 38 — Supporters' club organiser, 80+ members across multiple cities
**Motivation:** Runs a comp that feels like "their" comp — not a generic public game.
**Pain point:** Current tools force a giant public leaderboard (members feel lost) or a chaotic WhatsApp spreadsheet system. Can't sub-divide her group into city chapters.
**How TribePicks solves it:** Tribe sub-groups for London / Manchester / Sydney chapters within one master comp. Members see their city leaderboard AND overall comp simultaneously. Announcement board for match-day posts. One link to share.
**Primary channel:** Facebook Groups, WhatsApp, direct email.

## 3. Channel Strategy

### Channel 1 — LinkedIn
**Rationale:** Direct line to comp organisers: HR managers, office managers, team leads, social committee members.
**Content type:**
- Thought leadership: "How to run a World Cup competition for your team without the spreadsheet chaos"
- Carousel: "5 things your office World Cup tipping comp should have in 2026"
- 30–60 sec product demo video
- Sponsored InMail to HR/People & Culture job titles at 50–500 person companies

**Cadence:** 3–4 organic posts/week from May. 1–2 paid campaigns from late May through opening week.

### Channel 2 — Instagram Reels + Stories
**Rationale:** Reaches casual fans (Persona 2) and 18–35 demographic that engages with football content socially.
**Content type:**
- Reels: "30-second challenge — make your 4 picks, see your rank" (/su-challenge screen-capture walkthrough)
- Stories: countdowns, poll stickers ("Who's winning the World Cup?"), swipe-up to challenge link
- Collab posts with football influencer accounts

**Cadence:** 1 Reel/week April–May; 3–4/week during tournament. 5 Stories/week throughout.

### Channel 3 — WhatsApp / SMS Viral Loop
**Rationale:** The /su-challenge page is the strongest acquisition tool. Natural sharing surface is direct message — zero cost, person-to-person.
**Content type:**
- Pre-written copy templates for organisers to forward (see Section 6)
- QR code asset for existing WhatsApp groups
- SMS campaign for registered users to invite their networks

**Cadence:** Template release at challenge launch, at tournament draw, and in opening week.

### Channel 4 — Google Search (SEM + SEO)
**Rationale:** High-intent search volume around "World Cup tipping competition" spikes hard from May. Capture organisers actively researching solutions.
**Target keywords:** "world cup tipping competition", "football prediction game 2026", "office world cup competition", "world cup sweepstake alternative"
**Content:** Organiser-focused landing page + blog content for long-tail SEO

**Cadence:** Paid search mid-May through end of group stage. SEO content published April–May for indexing time.

### Channel 5 — Reddit & Football Communities
**Rationale:** r/soccer (17M+ members), r/worldcup, and national team subreddits contain Persona 3 users — football obsessives who become power users and evangelists.
**Content type:**
- "Show HN"-style product posts (authentic, feature-forward, no hard sell)
- Organic comments in "World Cup prediction game" threads linking to /su-challenge

**Cadence:** 1–2 organic posts in late May at tournament buzz peak.

## 5. Campaign Phases

### Phase 1 — Pre-Tournament Acquisition (Now → 10 June 2026)
**Primary action:** Drive comp creation and challenge completions.
- Organiser targeting: LinkedIn ads and content, Google search ads live mid-May
- Challenge seeding: Distribute /su-challenge link via social and influencer partnerships
- Email warm-up: Existing users receive "invite your friends / start a comp" prompts
- SEO content published (organiser how-to guides, platform comparison content)

### Phase 2 — Tournament Launch (11–15 June 2026)
**Primary action:** Convert challenge completions to registered accounts. Drive first-match tips.
- "Start your comp before the first whistle" urgency messaging
- Instagram/TikTok Reels: opening match reaction content showing leaderboard shifts
- Push/email to all registered users: "Your first match tips are due"
- PR: pitch "World Cup office comp guide" to HR publications

### Phase 3 — Group Stage Engagement (16 June – 2 July 2026)
**Primary action:** Retain tipsters, reduce tip abandonment, sustain organiser NPS.
- Match-day email/push automated reminders
- Leaderboard drama content: "Top of your comp after 10 matches? Here's who's chasing you"
- Instagram Stories: daily match previews with engagement polls
- Reddit: ongoing presence in match-day discussion threads

### Phase 4 — Knockout Stage Re-Engagement (3 July – Final, ~19 July 2026)
**Primary action:** Re-activate lapsed tipsters; drive shootout prediction awareness.
- "The real competition starts now" messaging — shootout predictions live
- Win-back email to users who haven't tipped in 7+ days
- High-production match preview graphics and bracket visualisations
- Post-tournament: winner celebration + "Start a comp for the next tournament" CTA

## 6. The Warm-Up Challenge as an Acquisition Hook (/su-challenge)

### Why This Is Your Best Acquisition Asset

The /su-challenge page requires zero sign-up, delivers value (a projected rank) in under 60 seconds, and is inherently shareable. Every challenge completion is a warm lead.

**Funnel:** share link → complete challenge → see projected rank → register to lock in picks → join/create a comp

### Mini-Campaign Plan

1. **Seed with existing organisers (early May)** — Email every organiser with the WhatsApp copy below + a comp invite link
2. **Social seeding (mid-May → tournament start)** — Post challenge link natively; pin to profiles; add to link-in-bio
3. **Viral loop** — Post-challenge screen shows projected rank prominently + one-tap share button with pre-written copy: *"I just ranked X out of Y on the TribePicks World Cup challenge — can you beat me?"*
4. **WhatsApp/SMS seeding by organisers** — Arm organisers with ready-to-send copy (below)
